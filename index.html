The "Taiwan Louis Vuitton"

Practical, iconic, and retro-chic—the red, green, and blue striped 茄芷袋 (Ga Ji Dai) has evolved from a simple grocery tote used by grandmas into one of Taiwan's most celebrated fashion statements and must-buy souvenirs.

What is a Ga Ji Bag?

Sometimes fondly referred to as the "Grandma Bag" (阿嬤袋) or jokingly called the "Taiwan LV Bag" (Louis Vuitton, but much cheaper!), the iconic striped tote bag features a distinct pattern of red, green, and blue woven mesh.

Originally born as a practical tool for daily errands, farming, and grocery shopping, these bags have transcended their humble working-class origins to become a globally recognized symbol of Taiwanese heritage and retro-chic design.

The Story & Origins

Japanese Colonial Era
Handwoven Plant Fibers in Tainan

The origins trace back to Jingliao village in Houbi District, Tainan. Local farmers hand-wove the first bags using triangular rush grass (Cyperus malaccensis) to carry seeds and earn extra household income.

1960s
Nylon Revolution & Tricolor Mesh

During Taiwan’s industrial expansion, production shifted to machine-sewn nylon mesh. The signature red, green, and blue colors emerged, making the bags lightweight, durable, quick-drying, and virtually indestructible.

1980s – 2000s
From Decline to Nostalgic Revival

As modern plastic bags and leather goods rose, Ga Ji bags fell out of mainstream favor for two decades. However, boosted by documentary films and cultural preservation efforts in Jingliao, the bags sparked a powerful nostalgic comeback.

Present Day
A Modern Fashion & Cultural Icon

Today, Ga Ji bags are embraced by both old and young generations, as well as international tourists. They have even appeared at international fashion shows as a symbol of Taiwanese culture!

🛍️ Where to Buy Your Ga Ji Bag in Taiwan

  • Dihua Street (迪化街, Taipei): The best spot for authentic market vibes. Standard bags usually cost between NT$60 – NT$90 depending on size.
  • Ximending (西門町, Taipei): Great for trendy redesigned souvenirs (like drink holders and zip pouches), though tourist prices can be slightly higher.
  • Jingliao Old Street (後壁菁寮老街, Tainan): Visit the historic birthplace of Ga Ji bags to see original artisan workshops!
